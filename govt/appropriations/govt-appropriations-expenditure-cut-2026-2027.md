### Background
In this blogpost I'm going to propose specific ways in which Kenya can reduce government expenditure. This is the FY **2026/2027** edition; the previous edition (`govt-appropriations-expenditure-cut-2025-2026.md`) targeted the 2025/2026 appropriations.

A few caveats;
1. This presumes that we do need to reduce government expenditure. If you think we shouldn't, this is probably not the blogpost for you.
2. This analysis is based on the FY 2026/2027 budget appropriations books published on Kenya's National Treasury website (https://www.treasury.go.ke/index.php/budget-books). Those books might not yet contain amendments made in parliament. You can also get them from the parliament website (https://www.parliament.go.ke/).
3. In my analysis I either cut out a whole budget item or I leave it as is. I do **not** go into the details of trying to *reduce* certain allocations. As an example, the Government Press is allocated KES 500m for "Modernization of Press & Refurbishment of Buildings" in 2026/27. I will always choose either (a) leave the allocation as is, or (b) cut the whole allocation; I never choose (c) reduce the allocation. This article is about things I would tentatively *eliminate* from the appropriations bill, not things I would trim/amend.
4. These are my opinions and I have no doubt you have yours that differ. That's fine.

**Two additional filters I applied this year (explicit):**
- **The "post‑2021" filter.** I flag offices/state departments/functions that were *created on any date after 1 January 2021*. The single biggest source of these is the Executive Order reorganisations of 2022/2023, which manufactured a swarm of new state departments (and the unconstitutional Office of the Prime Cabinet Secretary). New != useful. A function that the Republic managed without until 2022 is, by definition, a function the Republic can survive without.
- **The "public goods" filter.** Economics has a precise definition of a *public good*: it is **non‑rival** (my consumption doesn't reduce yours) and **non‑excludable** (you can't easily be kept from using it). National defence, the rule of law, public health surveillance, basic research, macroeconomic stability and the *core* road network are close to genuine public goods — government is the right provider. House‑building, tourism marketing, running slaughterhouses, "investment promotion", trading maize, film schools and "brand" campaigns are **private/club goods** — rival and excludable — and the market provides them without a line item. I target the private goods. **Exception:** I retain some non‑public goods where they are genuine *natural monopolies* (a single network is cheaper than competing ones), e.g. the national electricity grid, trunk water/sewer networks, and the core road network. Building duplicate versions of these would be wasteful, so the state stays.

**A word on the numbers.** Per the book's own convention, the "gross"/"total" figures are inclusive of Appropriations‑in‑Aid (AIA = grants, loans, and fees the agency collects itself). Where a programme is funded almost entirely from AIA (e.g. a levy, or an agency's own fees), *cutting it does not save the Exchequer a shilling* — it instead means **repealing a levy** or shutting a self‑financing shop. I flag those explicitly so nobody double‑counts the savings.

### The fiscal backdrop (why any of this matters)
From the FY 2026/2027 books:

- **Total Voted Expenditure** (all MDAs, recurrent + development, inclusive of AIA): **KES 2,817,388,223,249** (~KES 2.82 trillion).
  - Recurrent voted: KES 1,976,778,899,705
  - Development voted: KES 840,609,323,544
- **Consolidated Fund Services (CFS):** **KES 2,562,973,919,672** (~KES 2.56 trillion)
  - Public Debt (interest + redemptions, internal + external): **KES 2,315,884,392,206**
  - Pensions and Gratuities: KES 241,937,772,895
  - Salaries and Allowances (constitutional office holders): KES 5,079,274,572
  - Miscellaneous Services: KES 72,480,000
- **GRAND TOTAL (voted + CFS):** **KES 5,380,362,142,921** (~KES 5.38 trillion).

Sit with that for a second. **Public debt service alone (KES 2.32t) is bigger than the entire development budget (KES 0.84t) — about 2.8× bigger — and equals ~82% of everything the entire government of Kenya votes for all its ministries combined.** We are not primarily a country with a spending problem in the ministries; we are a country whose past borrowing now eats the present. That is the argument for *both* austerity on the voted side *and* a debt re‑profiling (see the "things to look at" section). Everything below is the voted‑side contribution.

> Note on year‑on‑year figures: I quote the 2026/27 estimate for each item. Where I compare to 2025/26, I use the *restated baseline* printed inside the 2026/27 book (which reflects supplementary revisions), so it will sometimes differ from the figure quoted in last year's edition of this article.

### Offices / State Departments created after 1 January 2021 (quick‑reference)
These are prime candidates on the "post‑2021" filter alone, before we even ask what they do:

- **Office of the Prime Cabinet Secretary** (Vote 1013) — created by Executive Order in 2022; it has no basis in the Constitution. FY2026/27: **KES 839,790,954**.
- **State Department for National Government Coordination** (Vote 1018) — a shell to pad the PCS office. FY2026/27: **KES 1,062,305,141**. (It literally runs the *same* programme code — 0755000 "Government Coordination and Supervision" — as the PCS office. See item 2.)
- **State Department for Parliamentary Affairs** (Vote 1014) — Executive Order 1 of 2023. FY2026/27: **KES 361,201,100**.
- **State Department for Cabinet Affairs** (Vote 1016) — Executive Order 1 of 2023. FY2026/27: **KES 269,386,723**.
- **State Department for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development** (Vote 1176) — Executive Order 1 of 2023. FY2026/27: **KES 5,339,502,500**.
- **State Department for Investment Promotion** (Vote 1177). FY2026/27: **KES 5,017,252,723**.
- **State Department for Diaspora Affairs** (Vote 1054). FY2026/27: **KES 817,268,920**.
- **State Department for Science, Innovation and Research** (Vote 1067) — the department itself states it "was not operational during the review period of FY 2021/22 to 2023/24". FY2026/27: **KES 1,256,127,567**.

Each is dealt with on its merits below, but the burden of proof should be on them to justify existing at all.

### Suspicious government departments / offices / programmes
These are the ones I think we can look at if we want to reduce expenditure.

1. **Executive Office of the President (Vote 1011) / State House (Vote 1017)**
   1. **Government Printing Services — KES 1,292,237,031** (of which KES 792,237,031 recurrent and **KES 500,000,000** development for "Modernization of Press & Refurbishment of Buildings at GP").
      Abolish the Government Printer (Government Press / GP; https://www.governmentpress.go.ke/background) and replace it with a **"Kenya Gazette website"**.
      The Constitution defines "Gazette" as "the Kenya Gazette published by authority of the national government, or a supplement to the Kenya Gazette" (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng@2010-09-03#chp_Seventeen__sec_260). A website meets that definition.
      The replacement website needs a budget to build and run, but nowhere near KES 1.29b. Based on my experience building and running software, that expense is small enough not to move the calculations here. It would require amending laws that assume physical printing, e.g. the Revision of the Laws Act: "The Laws of Kenya shall be printed in the form of booklets..." (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1979/9/eng@2022-12-31#sec_6).
      *Public‑goods note:* the *official record* (the Gazette) is a legitimate state function; the *printing press monopoly* is not. Digitise the former, kill the latter. Note the refurbishment line ballooned from ~KES 177m (2025/26) to KES 500m — a monument being renovated for a task a URL performs.
   2. **General Works at the Office of the Deputy President — KES 100,000,000.** State House Nairobi has KES 530,400,000 of maintenance/refurbishment this year; the two offices can share that.
   3. **Presidential residences (development):**
      - General Maintenance Works at Eldoret State Lodge — KES 40,000,000
      - General Maintenance Works at State House Sagana — KES 21,300,000
      - Refurbishment of buildings at Mombasa State House — KES 201,100,000
      - Refurbishment of buildings at Nakuru State House — KES 98,700,000
      - Rehabilitation Works at Kisumu State Lodge — KES 90,000,000
      - Rehabilitation Works at Kakamega State Lodge — KES 20,000,000
      - Kisii State Lodge — KES 12,600,000
      - Nairobi State House: Purchase of Household Furniture and Institutional Equipment — KES 31,200,000
      - Nairobi State House: ICT Purchase of Specialised Plant, Equipment and Machinery — KES 46,700,000

      **Recurrent:**
      - State House – Mombasa — KES 27,489,370
      - State House – Nakuru — KES 35,233,744
      - State Lodges — KES 113,624,453

      I would leave intact the **KES 452,500,000** for refurbishment of buildings at Nairobi State House. Maintain **one** presidential official residence; many countries have exactly one (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence). (Note: the Mombasa and Nakuru refurbishment lines nearly *quadrupled* year‑on‑year — 42.5m→201.1m and 25m→98.7m — while debt service eats the budget.)
   4. **Public Entities Oversight Services — KES 398,229,423** (up from ~KES 277m). Evaluates state corporations, inducts board members, produces management audits. This duplicates the **State Department for Public Investments and Assets Management** (Vote 1073), whose programme *Public Investment & Portfolio Management* (KES 1,941,063,822) is "entrusted with the administration of public enterprises and review of performance". Pick one.

2. **The "Prime Cabinet Secretary" cluster — an unconstitutional office and its life‑support machines.**
   1. **Office of the Prime Cabinet Secretary (Vote 1013) — KES 839,790,954.** It is an unconstitutional office; it needs to be purged with a constitutional sword.
   2. **State Department for National Government Coordination (Vote 1018) — KES 1,062,305,141.** This department runs the **identical programme code (0755000, "Government Coordination and Supervision")** as the PCS office above — the clearest possible evidence that it exists to pad the PCS. It also duplicates the `State Department for Performance and Delivery Management` function of "Coordination and Supervision of Government". (It grew ~5× versus what last year's edition recorded.)
   3. **State Department for Parliamentary Affairs (Vote 1014) — KES 361,201,100.** Created by Executive Order 1 of 2023. Another appendage of the unconstitutional PCS office.
   4. **State Department for Cabinet Affairs (Vote 1016) — KES 269,386,723.** Created by Executive Order 1 of 2023. It "facilitates cabinet meetings and oversees implementation of cabinet decisions" (https://cabinetaffairs.go.ke/functions). The Cabinet already has a Secretary to the Cabinet, and every Cabinet member has dozens of staff.

3. **State Department for Devolution (Vote 1032) — KES 11,996,977,757.**
   Mission: to offer strategic leadership and policy direction in the governance of devolution and intergovernmental relations. There is significant overlap with the Senate, the Commission on Revenue Allocation, the Intergovernmental Budget and Economic Council, the Council of Governors, and the Intergovernmental Relations Technical Committee (igrtc.go.ke). We already have devolved functionaries (doctors, etc.) working in counties; what we lack is the *money* following those functions. A national department "eating" ~KES 12b a year to "facilitate" devolution is part of why devolution is unfinished. (It did shrink ~KES 3.5b year‑on‑year, largely donor programmes winding down — good; finish the job.)

4. **State Department for Special Programmes (Vote 1033) — KES 809,259,192** (Disaster Risk Management).
   Mission: promote disaster preparedness and help vulnerable communities. We already have the National Drought Management Authority (ndma.go.ke), the National Disaster Management Unit (disastermanagement.go.ke) and the National Disaster Operations Centre (ndoc.go.ke) — and near‑every county runs a statutory disaster fund (e.g. Kwale's is 2% of the annual county budget; https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke-002/act/2016/7/eng@2016-11-04). How many agencies do you need to fight the same disasters?

5. **State Department for the ASALs and Regional Development (Vote 1036) — KES 10,915,895,673.**
   Mission: strategic planning/development of Arid and Semi‑Arid Lands and basin‑based regions. The Constitution already provides the **Equalisation Fund** (0.5% of national revenue) "to provide basic services... to marginalised areas... to bring them to the level generally enjoyed by the rest of the nation" (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng@2010-09-03#chp_TWELVE__part_I__sec_204). That constitutional fund is untouched by this cut.

6. **State Department for Culture, The Arts and Heritage (Vote 1134).**
   - Culture/Heritage — KES 1,678,562,919
   - The Arts — KES 239,327,012
   - General Administration, Planning and Support Services — KES 159,957,641

   I'd keep **Public Records Management (KES 189,227,229)** and **Library Services (KES 462,656,209)** — the public archive and libraries are close to genuine public goods. New this year is a **"Lottery Control, Licensing and Regulations" programme (KES 292,052,248)**; regulation of gambling is a legitimate state function (an externality problem), so I retain it — but it deserves scrutiny as a fresh line.

7. **State Department for Youth Affairs and Creative Economy (Vote 1135) — KES 6,503,717,196** (up ~KES 2.3b YoY).
   Mission: improve youth employability, entrepreneurship and job creation. KPIs include training a few dozen youth in film and graduating a few dozen from a film school. Removing this also ends the Youth Enterprise Development Fund (fine, the Hustler Fund is retained). "Creative economy" and "mindset education" (they planned to put tens of thousands of youths through the latter) are **private/club goods**, not public goods — the market and civil society do this. I don't discount that they report generating employment; I dispute that a state department is the efficient vehicle for it.

8. **State Department for Investment Promotion (Vote 1177) — KES 5,017,252,723.**
   Mission: coordinate/promote/facilitate local, diaspora and foreign private investment; among other things it establishes EPZs. The right policy is to make **all of Kenya** friendly to business, not to run preferential enclaves for a chosen few. Keep the already‑gazetted EPZs; retire the department. "Investment promotion" benefits the specific investor courted — a private good.

9. **State Department for Gender and Affirmative Action (Vote 1212) — KES 6,261,403,526.**
   The **National Gender and Equality Commission** (https://www.ngeckenya.org/about/15/mandate) already exists and its mandate overlaps this department. (Note the department's biggest line is a KES 5.01b "Community Development" transfer programme — worth a hard look before it is treated as untouchable.)

10. **State Department for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Vote 1176) — KES 5,339,502,500.**
    Created by Executive Order 1 of 2023. Mission: financing, incubation and "entrepreneurship management and training" for MSMEs. Cutting it means we simply stop topping up the **Hustler Fund** with new money; the Fund continues to revolve on the ~KES 30b already capitalised over the last few years. MSME support to a specific enterprise is a private good; the enabling *environment* (contract enforcement, low taxes) is the public good, and that lives elsewhere.

11. **State Department for East African Community Affairs (Vote 1221) — KES 809,415,250.**
    Its role can be performed inside the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Neither Tanzania nor Rwanda maintains a standalone EAC ministry and they manage fine.

12. **Ministry rationalisation.** Where two of the state departments above sit under a single ministry, collapse the ministry accordingly (e.g. the EAC/ASALs & Regional Development pairing, and the Gender/Culture‑Arts‑Heritage pairing). The savings are captured in the department line items; the point is to also remove the duplicated ministerial overhead.

13. **State Department for Trade (Vote 1174)** — created by Executive Order 1 of 2023.
    1. Sub‑programme: **Export Trade Development, Promotion and National Branding — KES 692,241,390.** KPIs: help a handful of firms export "for the first time", brand products with a "made in Kenya" mark, run global marketing campaigns. Any Kenyan producer should be able to download and affix a "made in Kenya" mark without ministry facilitation.
    2. Sub‑programme: **Enabling Environment for Business Growth — KES 77,394,207.** KPIs: draft a bill, revise a policy, "capacity build 100 county employees on trade matters".
    3. Sub‑programme: **Development and Promotion of BETA Priorities Value Chains — KES 90,125,021.** KPIs: an e‑commerce policy, training sessions, a "digital services export strategy".
    4. **Kenya National Trading Corporation (KNTC) — KES 1,701,530,000 gross, but AIA = KES 1,701,530,000, so net Exchequer saving = KES 0.** Mission: "price stabilization of essential food items". KPIs: procure/distribute thousands of tonnes of maize. KNTC has failed at price stabilisation (a futile task), and price stability is constitutionally the **CBK's** mandate (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng@2010-09-03#chp_TWELVE__part_7__sec_231). What it *has* excelled at is losing money — see the ~KES 6b edible‑oil scandal (https://www.citizen.digital/news/oil-scandal-kntc-hard-pressed-to-explain-losing-ksh6-billion-taxpayers-money-n350965). Wind it up; the "saving" is stopping the losses, not the appropriation.

14. **State Department for Industry (Vote 1175).**
    Sub‑programme: **Industrial Development Promotion — KES 6,130,421,822.** Inter‑governmental forums, "policy reforms", SME profiles, and the County Aggregated Industrial Parks / agro‑industrial parks. Industrial parks that make sense will attract private capital; those that need permanent state subsidy are picking losers.

15. **State Department for Tourism (Vote 1202).**
    1. Sub‑programme: **Destination Marketing (Kenya Tourism Board) — KES 360,000,000.** Individual hotels/resorts/operators can — and do — market themselves globally, and can self‑organise for joint campaigns. Destination marketing benefits the specific businesses that get the bookings — a club good.
    2. Sub‑programme: **Tourism Promotion — KES 800,000,000.** Research studies, promotion materials, training forums.
    - I would **retain** the **Tourism Regulatory Authority (KES 800,000,000, wholly AIA‑funded)** — safety/standards regulation is a legitimate state function and it costs the Exchequer nothing. (Note: the *promotion* and *marketing* lines here total ~KES 1.16b, while TRA's regulatory line — the part worth keeping — is fully self‑financed.)

16. **State Department for Science, Innovation and Research (Vote 1067) — KES 1,256,127,567.**
    The department itself states it "was not operational during the review period of FY 2021/22 to 2023/24" — i.e. a post‑2021 creation. *Basic* research is a public good, but it is already funded through the universities, the National Research Fund and KENIA; a fresh coordinating department is overhead, not science.

17. **State Department for Diaspora Affairs (Vote 1054).**
    Sub‑programme: **Diaspora Investments, Remittances and International Jobs — KES 152,024,066** (up from ~KES 47m — a 3× jump). KPIs: investment expos and job fairs. Diaspora *welfare/consular* protection is a public function (and can sit in Foreign Affairs); running expos is not.

18. **State Department for Housing & Urban Development (Vote 1094).**
    Sub‑programme: **Delivery of Affordable and Social Housing Units — KES 112,284,200,000**, of which the **Affordable Housing Fund = KES 110,027,200,000 is entirely AIA from the Affordable Housing Levy.** Cutting this means **repealing the Affordable Housing Levy** — a direct KES ~110b/yr saving *to workers' payslips*, not to the Exchequer.
    A resource‑constrained state should not be building houses (a rival, excludable **private good**). People love saying "the private sector hasn't built houses", but between the 2009 and 2019 censuses the private sector (you and me) did enormous work: grass‑thatched roofs fell 13%→5%; earthen floors 56%→40%; fridge ownership 3%→10%. Housing improvement isn't concentrated where a politician can cut a ribbon, but it is real. Meanwhile, over the same period, *urban households with piped water* — a service government **is** meant to provide — fell 53%→42%. Fix the water; let people build their own homes.

19. **State Department for Public Works (Vote 1095).**
    1. **Construction of County Headquarters‑BETA — KES 535,000,000.** Why is the national government building headquarters for counties? A county that wants an HQ can pass a motion in its own assembly and budget for it.
    2. **Kenya Building Research Centre — KES 19,069,085.** Building research is best done by (already underfunded) universities; with the AHP programme wound down, this can go too.
    - *Retained (natural monopoly / public good):* trunk government office accommodation and coastline/flood‑protection works stay.

20. **State Department for Information Communication Technology & Digital Economy (Vote 1122).**
    1. **Konza.** Last year's edition kept the Konza spend *specifically because* it was funding "horizontal infrastructure to 100%" and committed to stopping any further Konza infrastructure funding **after FY2025/26**. That date has now arrived. In the 2026/27 book the horizontal‑infrastructure line (Vote 1122, "Horizontal Infrastructure Phase I – EPCF") is **KES 0**, yet KOTDA is still being fed: *Construction of Konza Complex Phase I B* (KES 175,401,640) and *Konza data Center & Smart City Facilities* (KES 210,000,000), plus a KOTDA capital transfer. Per the commitment I made last year, **these now go.** I do not believe in state‑planned cities; Konza should sink or swim on its own.
    2. **Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project — KES 4,338,000,000** (of which KES 1b is AIA/loan). This carries essentially the same KPIs (ward/school/government connectivity; thousands of public Wi‑Fi hotspots) as the **"Digital Superhighway" (KES 382,008,200)** line. Two programmes doing the same thing — eliminate the duplicate. *Retained (natural monopoly):* the national fibre backbone (NOFBI) itself.

21. **State Department for Broadcasting & Telecommunications (Vote 1123).**
    1. **News and Information Services — KES 4,614,608,426.** KPIs: hundreds of "news briefs", thousands of TV news items, monitoring reports on government websites. The state being in the news business is not important; anything worthwhile the government does, mass media (and social media) will disseminate for free.
    2. **Brand Kenya Initiative — KES 278,000,000.** KPI: produce a "Kenya Yearbook". What is this?
    3. **Kenya Broadcasting Corporation (KBC) — KES 2,297,600,000 (recurrent).** (Good news: the KBC development lines that existed last year — Analogue‑to‑Digital Migration and "Studio Mashinani" — are **KES 0** this year.) A few years ago KBC's MD committed the government to a KES 750b payout at the London Court of Arbitration without consulting the line ministry (https://nation.africa/kenya/news/kbc-managing-director-samuel-maina-sacked-over-sh750bn-payment-4468844) — roughly four years of the health budget — all so the national broadcaster can stream the World Cup in 480p once every four years. Exit the news‑dissemination business.

22. **State Department for Internal Security & National Administration (Vote 1026).**
    1. **Peace Building, National Cohesion and Values — KES 689,846,540.** Sensitising people on "climate‑change‑related conflicts" is not worthless, but with our resources it belongs on the back burner.
    2. **National Government Administration Coordination Services — KES 25,455,885,842.** This is the **Provincial Administration** (rebranded "National Government Administration"). The Sixth Schedule of the Constitution required that, "within five years... the national government shall restructure... the provincial administration to accord with and respect the system of devolved government" (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng@2010-09-03#att_6__part_4__para_17). Chiefs adjudicate some family/petty matters, but those roles are handled by the Judiciary, community/religious forums and the police; ID cards can be collected at Huduma Centres. Look how thin the National Government Co‑ordination Act is (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2013/1/eng@2024-05-17) — it doesn't even assign statutory duties to chiefs/county commissioners. For every role the provincial administration plays, a law‑mandated office already exists; let those offices do their work.

23. **Ministry of Defence (Vote 1041).**
    **Kenya Meat Commission — KES 300,000,000 (development) + KES 3,280,000,000 (recurrent).** The recurrent KES 3.28b is entirely AIA (KMC's own fees), so the **net Exchequer saving is the KES 300m development transfer**; the rest is "stop running a self‑financing loss‑maker". Either way, the defence ministry should have better things to do than run a slaughterhouse (a private, commercial good).

24. **State Department for Economic Planning (Vote 1072).**
    Sub‑programme: Community Development → **National Government Constituencies Development Fund (NGCDF) — KES 61,797,700,000.** The High Court declared CDF unconstitutional; the Court of Appeal overturned that. I would still remove it. This is a brutal political fight — repeal must go through the very MPs who are its chief beneficiaries — but this is my blogpost and I'm listing aspirations. (NGCDF *grew* ~KES 3b YoY even as the Treasury pleads poverty.)

25. **State Department for Roads (Vote 1091) — total KES 234,662,065,238.**
    Of this, the **Maintenance of Roads** sub‑programme (the Road Maintenance Levy transfer) is **KES 118,070,000,000**. The remainder — **Construction of Roads and Bridges (KES 48,100,610,917) + Rehabilitation of Roads (KES 64,168,539,161) + admin (KES 4,322,915,160) ≈ KES 116,592,065,238** — is funded from general taxes and borrowing. **Cut the non‑levy new‑build/rehab funding and let the department run on the ~KES 118b levy** (which funds construction *and* maintenance if disciplined).
    - *Public‑goods / natural‑monopoly note:* the *core road network* is retained precisely because it is a natural monopoly with large externalities — but it should be financed by the ring‑fenced levy (users pay), not by adding to the debt that already consumes KES 2.3t.
    - TODO: show the arithmetic that the levy alone suffices to construct new roads and maintain existing ones.

26. **State Department for Transport (Vote 1092).**
    1. **Nairobi Metropolitan Area Transport Authority (NAMATA) — KES 554,840,000.** NAMATA (Executive Order 18 of 2017) oversees public transport in the Nairobi metro. The Constitution makes county governments responsible for "county transport including county roads and public road transport" (Fourth Schedule; https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng@2010-09-03#att_4__part_2__para_5). NAMATA duplicates a county function.
    2. **Nairobi Bus Rapid Transit (BRT):**
       - Establishment of Bus Rapid Transit Line 5 Project (carried under Roads, Vote 1091) — KES 1,079,766,052
       - Nairobi Bus Rapid Transport Project‑BETA — KES 582,204,920
       - Public Transport Technical BRT Assistance Project (Phase 1) — KES 96,040,164

       I'm not opposed to a BRT in principle, but the pace (or lack of it) and the ideas thrown around make me doubt it's worth it. These lines *doubled/tripled* YoY for a project that keeps not moving.

27. **State Department for Energy (Vote 1152).**
    Sub‑programme: **Alternative Energy Technologies — KES 3,213,691,702.** Standalone solar home systems, tens of thousands of clean cookstoves, catchment reforestation, community mini‑hydro, institutional/domestic biogas. Worthy causes, but distributional handouts (private goods) rather than the public‑good core of the ministry. *Retained (natural monopolies/public goods):* the **National Grid System (KES 7.5b)** and **Rural Electrification (KES 16.3b)** — a single transmission/distribution network is the textbook natural monopoly and rural electrification has large externalities.

28. **The "AIDS Control Unit" epidemic (across almost every vote).**
    Dozens of unrelated MDAs each run a tiny "AIDS Control Unit" whose entire annual KPI is something like "conduct a few awareness sessions and test a couple hundred people". We already fund a whole Ministry of Health and a state corporation (the National Syndemic Diseases Control Council) for HIV/AIDS. Should every office also get a "Covid Control Unit"? Consolidate them into the health function. FY2026/27 examples:
    - Executive Office of the President — KES 322,200
    - Office of the Deputy President — KES 1,612,650
    - State House — KES 786,626
    - Ministry of Defence — KES 6,248,278
    - State Department for Foreign Affairs — KES 14,333,733
    - National Police Service — three separate units (KES 1,722,016 + 1,676,415 + 3,395,898)
    - State Department for Livestock Development — KES 7,288,997 (KPI: a handful of awareness sessions and ~200 tests)
    - ... and ~40 more departments/commissions.

    Across the MDAs these duplicative units total **≈ KES 142,542,715** (this *excludes* the genuine National AIDS/STI Control Programme of ~KES 115.3m under Medical Services, which is real public health and stays). Yes, both the *Office of the President* and *State House* run their own AIDS Control Units. Who approves these? Does anyone read the appropriations books?

### What changed since the 2025/2026 edition (analyst's notes)
Objectivity requires acknowledging where the government moved in the right direction and where it moved the wrong way:

- **Government already retreated on some fronts (good):** oil‑and‑gas exploration collapsed (KES 3.36b → KES 73m); KBC's development lines went to zero; Konza's "horizontal infrastructure" line went to zero; the Devolution and Petroleum votes shrank; total voted expenditure was roughly flat (‑0.7%).
- **Government doubled down on the wrong things (bad):** NGCDF grew ~KES 3b to KES 61.8b; the Provincial Administration line grew to KES 25.5b; the Youth Affairs vote grew ~KES 2.3b; State House Mombasa/Nakuru refurbishment lines roughly quadrupled; the Government Press "modernisation" line nearly tripled to KES 500m; "Management Consultancy Services" nearly tripled.
- **Reorganisation as camouflage:** agencies keep migrating between votes (MSEA, NCA, Kenya Leather Council, NYOTA, KMC), which frustrates year‑on‑year tracking and oversight. That opacity is itself an argument for a leaner, more legible structure.

### Indicative tally of potential savings (read the caveats)
I resist a single glossy "grand total" because the categories are not fungible. Broadly:

- **Direct net‑Exchequer savings** from cutting the departments/programmes above (excluding roads and excluding AIA/levy‑funded items): **≈ KES 179 billion/year.** This is dominated by a handful of big lines — NGCDF (KES 61.8b), the Provincial Administration (KES 25.5b), Devolution (KES 12b), ASALs (KES 10.9b) — plus the long tail of smaller departments and duplications.
- **Roads (non‑levy new‑build/rehab): up to ≈ KES 116.6 billion/year** removed from the tax‑and‑borrow envelope, leaving the sector to run on the ~KES 118b Road Maintenance Levy. Part of this is loan‑financed, so cutting it primarily reduces *new borrowing* rather than cash outlay — a debt‑stock saving, which is arguably what matters most.
- **Levy repeals (savings to citizens, ~zero to the Exchequer):** repealing the **Affordable Housing Levy** returns **≈ KES 110 billion/year** to workers' payslips; winding up KNTC (KES 1.7b AIA) and KMC's recurrent shop (KES 3.28b AIA) stops self‑financing loss‑makers.

So: on the order of **KES 180–300 billion** of annual voted spending is, in my judgement, eliminable without touching a single genuine public good — roughly **6–10% of Total Voted Expenditure**, or **~1–2% of GDP**. That is meaningful, but it also underscores the harder truth: the ministries are not where the trillions are. **The trillions are in debt service (KES 2.32t).** Austerity on the voted side buys credibility and breathing room; it does not, by itself, solve the arithmetic. That requires the debt work below.

### How to analyze Kenya budget books
- Get them from https://www.treasury.go.ke/index.php/budget-books; also use https://openbudget.or.ke/.
- There are six books/PDFs per year:
  1. Program‑Based Budget Book
  2. Development Budget Book Vol. 1
  3. Development Budget Book Vol. 2
  4. Development Budget Book Vol. 3
  5. Recurrent Budget Book Vol. 1
  6. Recurrent Budget Book Vol. 2
- In each book, search for `Total Voted Expenditure`.
  - The `Total Voted Expenditure` in each *Development* book is the same across all three volumes (it's the total for all development books, not per‑volume). Likewise for the two *Recurrent* volumes.
  - The `Total Voted Expenditure` in the *Program‑Based* book is the total of **both** development and recurrent.
- Reconciliation identity (worked example is in last year's edition): the Program‑Based total for a department = its Recurrent gross + its Development gross, and each gross already **includes** AIA (grants/loans/fees). So Program‑Based totals also include AIA.
- Terminology in the Program‑Based book:
  - `gross current estimates` = recurrent (current ≡ recurrent)
  - `gross capital estimates` = development (capital ≡ development)
  - `Appropriations in Aid (AIA)` = grants, loans, or fees collected by the agency.
- In the Program‑Based book, look under `CONSOLIDATED FUND SERVICES` for internal/external debt interest and redemptions, pensions, salaries & allowances of constitutional office holders, and subscriptions to international organisations.

**FY 2026/2027 headline numbers (from these books):**
- Total Voted Expenditure (recurrent + development, incl. AIA): **KES 2,817,388,223,249** (~2.82t)
  - Recurrent voted: KES 1,976,778,899,705
  - Development voted: KES 840,609,323,544
- Total Consolidated Fund Services: **KES 2,562,973,919,672** (~2.56t)
  - Public Debt: KES 2,315,884,392,206 (interest + redemptions, internal + external)
  - Pensions and Gratuities: KES 241,937,772,895
  - Salaries and Allowances (constitutional offices): KES 5,079,274,572
- **GRAND TOTAL (voted + CFS): KES 5,380,362,142,921** (~5.38t)
- To find the **Road Maintenance Levy**, look at the `Maintenance of Roads` sub‑programme under the State Department for Roads (the key outputs are "Transfer of receipts"). For 2026/27 it is **KES 118,070,000,000**.

**Road Maintenance Levy mechanics (unchanged structure):**
- KES 25/litre, split roughly: KES 15 (KeNHA 40%, KeRRA 32%, KURA 15%, KWS 1%, KRB 2%); KES 3 annuity fund (for PPPs); KES 7 securitised by KRB to clear pending bills (~KES 175b targeted over ~10 years). The KeRRA 32% is shared across constituencies; KRB's 2% is admin.
